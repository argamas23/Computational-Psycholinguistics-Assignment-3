The correlation analysis revealed three key relationships. Third, word frequency was negatively correlated with mean reading time (r = −0.12), indicating that more frequent words are read faster.

Together, these results show that reading time increases with word length and decreases with word frequency. However, the effect of length appears stronger than the effect of frequency in this dataset. These findings are consistent with well-established psycholinguistic effects: longer words require more visual and lexical processing, while high-frequency words benefit from faster lexical access.
